## Round 1 — LLD, DS, and a database argument

The round opened with a **low-level design of Zomato**. Not the full platform — the
interviewer scoped it to restaurant listing, menu, cart, and order placement. What they
were watching for was entity modelling and where behaviour lives: does `Order` know how
to price itself, or does a `PricingStrategy` own that? I walked through the classes,
the interfaces I'd keep open for extension (payment modes, delivery partners), and the
one place I'd allow a god-object smell for pragmatism.

Two warm-up DS questions followed, both classic:

- **Reverse Integer** — the whole question is the overflow check, not the reversal. I
  handled the 32-bit bound *before* the multiply, not after.
- **Palindrome Number** — solved without converting to a string, by reversing only half
  the digits and comparing.

Then the design half turned into the interesting part: **master/slave (primary/replica)
database design**. Replication lag, read-your-own-writes, why you route the write and
the immediately-following read to the primary, and what failover actually costs you.

That led into **two-phase commit** — the coordinator, the prepare phase, the commit
phase, and the honest answer about why 2PC is rarely what you want in practice: the
coordinator is a single point of failure and participants sit blocked holding locks
while it's down. I said I'd reach for a saga with compensating transactions before I'd
reach for 2PC.

## Round 2 — DSA

Two questions, both solved, both explained.

**Word Ladder.** BFS over the implicit graph where each word is a node and an edge
exists between words one character apart. The trick worth saying out loud is not the BFS
— it's how you generate neighbours. Don't compare every word against every other word
(`O(n² · L)`); instead build wildcard buckets like `h*t` → `[hot, hat, hit]` and look up
in `O(L · 26)`. I also mentioned bidirectional BFS as the optimisation if the word list
gets large.

**Heaters.** For each house, find the nearest heater and take the max of those distances
— that's your radius. Sort the heaters, then binary search each house's position to find
the two candidate heaters around it and take the closer one. `O((n + m) log m)`. I
walked through the edge cases: houses before the first heater and after the last one.

Both correct. Both explained with complexity. Rejected after this round.

## What I actually take from it

No feedback was given, so anything I say here is a guess — but two guesses feel honest:

1. **Correct isn't the bar at SDE-2.** Getting to the optimal solution is table stakes.
   The signal above it is how fast you get there, and how much of the reasoning happens
   out loud before the code rather than after it.
2. **The 2PC answer may have read as opinionated.** I argued against 2PC and for sagas.
   I still think that's right, but I pitched it as a conclusion instead of a trade-off,
   and in an interview the trade-off *is* the answer.

Writing this down mostly so future-me doesn't over-index on the rejection. The rounds
were fine. The gap was in delivery, not in the DSA.
