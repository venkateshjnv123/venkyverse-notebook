## Origin

I grew up in Andhra Pradesh, with a curiosity about how things work that eventually landed me in computer science. The interest started early — not from any grand ambition, just from noticing that software was the one thing where you could build something real without needing money or materials.

I joined IIT Jodhpur for B.Tech CSE in 2019. The first year was disorienting — algorithms felt abstract, and I wasn't sure I'd ever enjoy coding outside of problem sets. Second year flipped that. We started building actual systems — small services, real data — and something clicked. The satisfaction of watching a system handle real load felt different from anything else.

By third year, I was interning at Aspiro and accidentally falling in love with backend specifically. There's something about the invisibility of it — when you do it well, no one notices, but when you mess up, the whole product breaks. That kind of responsibility appealed to me.

## Current work

I joined Cars24 in 2023 as a backend engineer. The work centres around high-volume data flows — vehicle listings, pricing pipelines, event-driven workflows that need to stay consistent under load.

The interesting work has been on scaling event pipelines as inventory grew, redesigning webhook handling to deal with out-of-order delivery, and introducing smarter caching strategies at multiple layers. Most days are a mix of writing and reviewing code, debugging things that broke, and trying to predict the next thing that might break.

I'm on the SDE-2 track — which in practice means taking ownership of larger systems, helping newer engineers get unstuck faster, and being expected to make architecture decisions that hold up over time. The shift from *how* to *why* is the most interesting part of that progression.

## How I work with AI

Every engineer on LinkedIn is debating whether to use AI. I find that debate boring — the more interesting question is *how* you use it.

Here's roughly how I work: I reach for AI when I need to scaffold something fast, explore an unfamiliar library, or generate test cases for edge conditions I'd otherwise miss. I don't reach for it when making architecture decisions, debugging subtle race conditions, or figuring out what's worth building in the first place.

What AI does well for me: first drafts, boilerplate, documentation, test generation. What it doesn't do for me: production context, system topology awareness, deciding which tradeoffs matter.

The pattern I've found that works is this — AI handles the shape, I handle the intent. AI writes the first version, I do the critical read. The lesson I keep relearning is that AI generates working code, but working ≠ production-ready. That gap is where the senior engineer still earns their salary.
